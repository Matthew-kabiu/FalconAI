# Crop calendar: growth stage timings and sources

**Purpose:** source material for Falcon AI's crop calendar. After a farmer enters a crop and planting date, the app lists growth stages and when to expect them, counted in days after planting.

**Date compiled:** 2026-11-19

**Rule:** every number below has a citation (the `[n]` refers to the Sources list at the end). Nothing is estimated or averaged. **All of it is marked for review by an agronomist before farmers rely on it.**

How to read the tables:
- "Days" columns keep the source's own unit (days, weeks or months). Where a conversion is shown it is marked `≈` and uses 1 week = 7 days and 1 month = 30 days. That conversion is ours, not the source's.
- Some sources count from **emergence** (seedlings showing above the soil), not from **planting**. This is labelled wherever it applies. Emergence is usually a few days after planting, and the sources do not state that gap for most crops.
- Pesticide and fertiliser doses are deliberately left out. A timed activity such as "top-dress" is kept, but without the amount.
- Where two sources disagree, both figures are given.

## 1. Maize

| Stage | Days after planting (min–max) | Notes (variety/zone) | Source ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germination / emergence | not stated | — | — |
| Leaf growth (vegetative); critical weed-free period | first 4–6 weeks **after emergence** | applies to all maize | [1] p.330; [2] |
| Top-dressing stage | "knee high" [2]; or "2–3 weeks after planting, when the crop is about 45 cm high … optimum around the 8–10 leaf stage" [1] | the two KALRO wordings differ; reviewer should check | [1] p.338; [2] |
| Tasselling / silking (flowering) | **not found in a Kenyan source** | — | — |
| Grain filling | not stated | — | — |
| Physiological maturity / dry harvest | Extra early: 85–95 days (KCB, dryland mid-altitude) [1] p.57; 90–105 days (WSQ104, 0–1200 m) [1] p.15 | lowland / dry mid-altitude varieties | [1] |
| | Medium: 110–120 days (Coast Composite) [1] p.11; 120 days (Shukran-16, coastal lowlands) [1] p.30; 120–150 days (several hybrids) [1] | varies by hybrid | [1], [2] |
| | Highland (1600–2500 m): 135–150 days (H624, 1600–2300 m) [1] p.289; 140–160 days (KH600-11D, 1800–2500 m) [1] p.196; 160–185 days (H6210, 1800–2500 m) [1] p.278 | highland hybrids | [1] |
| | Very cold highlands (2200–3000 m): HAC 140–160 days [1] p.204; "commercial highland varieties take up to 13 months" there [1] p.203–204 | the same page also says HAC "takes 7–8 months"; the source contradicts itself | [1] |

**Timed activities**
- First hand weeding "at least / latest 3 weeks after emergence", second weeding 3 weeks later [1] p.330; [2]. The portal also says "Timely weeding is most effective at 2–3 weeks after sowing … second weeding 2 to 3 weeks later" [2].
- Weed and thin **before** top-dressing [1] p.338; [2].
- Green maize: harvest when the silk is dry and has turned black [2]. Dry maize: harvest when most leaves are dry, husks are dry and there is a black layer at the kernel base [1] p.342; [2]. The source gives no day count for either.

**Confidence: medium.** Maturity by variety and zone is well documented by KALRO. The flowering and grain-fill stages have no citable Kenyan day count.

**Gaps:** days to tasselling, silking and grain fill. The KEPHIS maize list [6] (pp.45–120) was not extracted; it could cross-check maturity per hybrid.

## 2. Sorghum

| Stage | Days after planting (min–max) | Notes (variety/zone) | Source ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergence | not stated | — | — |
| Vegetative (weeding, thinning) | weeding "two weeks after germination"; thinning "after 3 weeks" | general practice | [3] p.57 |
| Flowering | not stated in days; second weeding and top-dress "just before flowering" | — | [4] |
| Physiological maturity (hard dough) | "75–90 days" (general statement) | — | [4] |
| | Lowland / mid-altitude: Gadam 3 months (250–1600 m) [3] p.6; Seredo 3 months [3] p.25 vs 4 months [6] p.138; Serena 3 months [6] p.138; E97 90 days [3] p.31; KARI Mtama-1 3–3.5 (4) months [3] p.11 | low to mid altitude | [3], [6] |

**Timed activities**
- Weeding 2 weeks after germination; thinning after 3 weeks to one plant per hole [3] p.57.
- Second weeding and top-dressing just before flowering [4].
- Harvest at physiological maturity (dough stage, kernels past milk stage). Bird scaring and harvest at dough stage are listed as bird-damage measures [3] p.57; [4].

**Confidence: medium.** Kenyan maturity data are good. Flowering has no day count, and KALRO and KEPHIS disagree on some varieties.

**Gaps:** days to flowering (booting/heading); the conflicts noted above.

## 3. Finger millet (and pearl millet)

| Stage | Days after planting (min–max) | Notes (variety/zone) | Source ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergence | not stated | — | — |
| Vegetative (weeding, thinning, top-dress) | 1st weeding 2 weeks **after emergence**; thin, gap and top-dress after the 1st weeding; 2nd weeding 2 weeks after the 1st | — | [5] |
| Flowering / heading | not stated | — | — |
| Physiological maturity | Very early: EUFM-401 65–75 days; MSU FM 60D 80–90 days; Kis-Wimbi 1 81–100 days; KAT FM 1 80–115 days | lowland dry areas up to 1200–1500 m | [5] |
| | Medium: P-224 95–125 days; Kak-Wimbi 5 86–135 days; Kak-Wimbi 6 87–118 days; EUFM-502 100–120 days; EUFM-503 90–98 days | low to medium/high altitude | [5] |
| | Late: Kak-Wimbi 4 87–130 days; Nakuru FM-1 120–210 days (cool highlands) | Nakuru FM-1: 1750–2300 m, 5–7 months in [6] | [5]; [6] p.132 |
| **Pearl millet** maturity | Kat/PM 1: 2–3 months; Kat/PM 2 and 3: 2 months (250–1150 m) | KEPHIS only | [6] p.137 |

**Timed activities** (finger millet) [5]
- First weeding 2 weeks after emergence, then thin and gap to about 15 cm, then top-dress when the soil is moist.
- Second weeding 2 weeks after the first.
- Harvest at physiological maturity: a pinched grain no longer crushes into a milky substance. Delay causes shattering.
- Dry the heads 4–5 days after harvest.

**Confidence: medium** (finger millet; maturity per variety is good). **Low** (pearl millet; maturity only).

**Gaps:** days to heading for both millets; any field activities for pearl millet.

## 4. Rice

| Stage | Days after planting (min–max) | Notes (variety/zone) | Source ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nursery (transplanted rice) | seedlings ready 16 days after sowing; transplant before 25 days | **not Kenya-specific** (Uganda) | [19] p.23 |
| | 20–30 days in nursery (short/medium duration); 20–40 days (long duration) | **not Kenya-specific** (IRRI general) | [20] Step 5 |
| Vegetative (germination → panicle initiation) | 60 days | **not Kenya-specific**; the source gives one generic value | [19] p.6 |
| Panicle initiation | 65–70 days after sowing (top-dress timing) | **not Kenya-specific** | [19] p.24 |
| Reproductive (panicle initiation → heading) | 30 days | **not Kenya-specific** | [19] p.6 |
| Ripening (heading → maturity) | 30 days | **not Kenya-specific** | [19] p.6 |
| | "Most varieties take 50–55 days from panicle initiation to harvest"; harvest about 30 days after flowering | IRRI general | [20] |
| Maturity (Kenya-registered varieties) | NERICA 1/4/11: 3–3.4 months; NERICA 10: 2–3 months; NIBAM 10/11/110: 3–4 months; NIBAM 108: 5–6 months; NIBAM 109: 4–5 months; hybrids 3–5 months | 15–1700 m; Basmati entry is "ND" (no data) | [6] p.153 |

**Timed activities**
- Replant missing hills 10 days after transplanting [19] p.24.
- Upland rice: weed at least twice, at 3 and 6 weeks after germination [19].
- Lowland: first weeding within 2–3 weeks after establishment, second 2–3 weeks later [20] Step 6.
- Top-dress at panicle initiation [19] p.24.
- Harvest when 80–85% of grains are straw-coloured, about 30 days after flowering [20].

**Confidence: low.** The stage timings come from Uganda (NaCRRI/JICA) and IRRI general guidance, not from a Kenyan (Mwea/KALRO) source. Only maturity per variety is Kenya-specific.

**Gaps:** no KALRO or Mwea (MIAD) rice manual was found online; Basmati 370 maturity (the main Mwea variety) has no KEPHIS value.

## 5. Common beans

| Stage | Days after planting (min–max) | Notes (variety/zone) | Source ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergence / gapping | gapping 2 weeks after planting | — | [7] |
| Vegetative | 3–4 weeks after planting (≈21–28 days) | stated as "vegetative stage" | [7] |
| Flowering and early podding | 5–6 weeks after planting (≈35–42 days) | — | [7] |
| Maturity (bush beans) | KAT B1, KK Red 16: 2.5 months; KatX56, Nyota, Angaza, Faida, Rosecoco: 2.5–3 months; GLPx92: 2–3 months | 900–2000 m depending on variety | [7] |
| | KEPHIS list: 2–4 months across rows 1–40 (e.g. Mwitemani 2–3 months at 900–1600 m; "Canadian" 3–3.5 months; Mbigo 3–4 months) | — | [6] p.167 |
| Maturity (climbing) | Kenya Mavuno (Mac 64): 3–5 months, 1400–2000 m | climbing bean | [7] |

**Timed activities** [7]
- Gap-fill 2 weeks after planting.
- Mulch 2–3 weeks after emergence.
- Pure stand: first weeding 2–3 weeks after emergence, second 3 weeks later (just before flowering). Intercrop: one weeding 3 weeks after planting. Do not weed during flowering.
- Climbing beans: stake within 1–2 weeks after emergence.
- Harvest when all pods have turned yellow, before they shatter.

**Confidence: high.** Kenyan KALRO source, consistent with KEPHIS.

**Gaps:** days to emergence.

|---|---|---|---|
| Sprouting / establishment | replace cuttings that have not budded 2 weeks after planting | — | [12] |
| Early growth (weed control) | weed control needed "for the first three months of growth" | — | [12] |
| Maturity (harvest) | general: 8–18 months after planting | — | [12] |
| | Early (lowland coast, 15–1200 m): Tajirika, Karembo 8 months; Shibe, Siri, Karibuni 8–12 months; Nzalauka 6–8 months | — | [6] p.24 |
| | Semi-arid east: KME2/3/4 8–10 months | — | [6] p.24 |

| | KALRO portal per variety: 6–8 up to 14–16 months | — | [12] |

**Timed activities** [12]
- Plant when 2 months of adequate soil moisture is expected.
- Gap-fill 2 weeks after planting.
- Weed with hoes "3 or more times" (no dates given).

- A sign of maturity is radial cracking of the soil around the stem.

**Confidence: medium.** Maturity is well documented; there are no intermediate growth stages.

**Gaps:** stage timings between establishment and harvest; dated weeding rounds.

## 10. Irish potatoes

| Stage | Days after planting (min–max) | Notes (variety/zone) | Source ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sprout development + vegetative growth (stages 1–2) | together "roughly 4 to 10 weeks" (≈28–70 days) | depends on environment, seed age and variety | [13] p.24 |
| Tuber set (initiation) | follows stage 2; "flowering starts at the end of this stage" | no separate day count | [13] p.24 |
| Tuber bulking | "can last for more than 1.5 months" | — | [13] p.24 |
| Maturation | vines yellow; dehaulm (cut off vines) at this stage | — | [13] p.24; [14] |
| Maturity (days after planting) | Asante, Kenya Mpya, Kenya Baraka, Shangi, Purple Gold, Markies, Connect, Kerr's Pink: 90–120 days; Tigoni, Dutch Robijn, Sherekea, Jelly and others: >120 days | table credited to NPCK 2015 | [13] p.10 |
| | KEPHIS: 2–6 months overall (Kerr's Pink, Roslin Tana 2–3 months; Shangi 3–4 months; Dutch Robijn 4–5 months; Destiny, Kuroda 6 months) | 1400–3000 m depending on variety | [6] p.27 |

**Timed activities**
- First weeding and first hilling (earthing up) 2 weeks after full emergence; second hilling 2 weeks after the first [14].
- APNI wording: first hilling at 75% emergence (about 10–20 days after sprouts emerge), second 2–3 weeks later at flower-bud formation [13] p.13.
- First top-dress one month after planting (10–20 days after sprouts emerge); second at flower-bud formation [13] p.13. NPCK says top-dress at flowering [14].
- Stop irrigation and dehaulm 2 weeks before harvest [13] p.14; [14].

**Confidence: high.** Kenya-specific sources (APNI's Kenya guide citing NPCK, NPCK itself, KEPHIS).

**Gaps:** days to emergence (it depends on sprouting and seed age).

## 11. Sweet potatoes

| Stage | Days after planting (min–max) | Notes (variety/zone) | Source ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. Establishment | weeks 0–3 (≈0–27 days): fast root growth; storage roots start to differentiate around week 2 | **East Africa (CIP), not Kenya-specific** | [15] p.14 |
| Storage root initiation | begins "as early as two to three weeks", on average 4–6 weeks after planting | — | [15] p.13 |
| II. Intermediate (vine growth, root initiation) | weeks 4–7 (≈28–55 days) | — | [15] p.14 |
| III. Final phase (root bulking) | from week 8; "by 8–12 weeks after planting all energy is devoted to the bulking of the storage roots" | — | [15] pp.13–14 |
| Harvest | general: 3–8 months after planting; earliest varieties about 3 months, others 4.5–5 months | — | [16] p.250; [15] |
| | Kenya varieties: lowland/coast Mtwapa 8, 22/77: 3.5 months; Jayalo 4 months; mid-altitude (1200–2000 m) Kabode, Vitaa, SPK 004, Kemb 10: 3–5 months; highland Kenspot 1–5 (1700–2300 m): 6–7 months | altitude strongly affects maturity | [6] p.11 |

**Timed activities** [15] p.14
- Gap-fill in week 1.
- Weed in the intermediate phase (week 4); keep weeds controlled for the first two months.
- Lift vines at week 8 and hill up around week 11.
- The example table shows harvest at week 14. Piecemeal harvest (lifting a few roots at a time) is common.

**Confidence: medium.** The stages come from CIP's East African training manual, not a Kenyan publisher. Maturity by zone is Kenyan (KEPHIS).

**Gaps:** a KALRO-published stage table.

## 12. Bananas

| Stage | Days after planting (min–max) | Notes (variety/zone) | Source ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sucker production | about 2 months after planting | — | [17] |
| Flowering (shooting) | 8–12 months after planting | "depending on the climate and management" | [17] |
| Fruit filling | fruit matures 3–4 months **after flowering** | FHIA-23 has the shortest flowering-to-harvest period (96 days) | [17]; [18] PDF p.48 |
| Harvest (maturity) | tissue-culture plants: 9–12 months; conventional suckers: 12–14 months | — | [18] PDF p.102 |

**Timed activities** [17]
- Desuckering: select 3 suckers, then 1–2 months later keep the most vigorous and remove the rest within two months.
- Remove dried flower remains 8–11 days after the bunch emerges.
- Weed regularly until the leaf canopy suppresses weeds.
- Harvest when fingers are three-quarters round, light green and shiny.

**Confidence: medium.** KALRO sources; the first crop only. Ratoon cycles differ, and timing is not given per variety.

**Gaps:** maturity per variety; altitude effects.
